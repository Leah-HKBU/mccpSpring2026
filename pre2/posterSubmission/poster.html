Introduction

Serendipity means the recommended item is a pleasant surprise to the user. It plays a pivotal role in enhancing user satisfaction within recommender systems (Chen et al., WWW 2019), yet its evaluation poses significant challenges due to its inherently subjective nature and conceptual ambiguity.

Key Findings

  • Zero-shot LLMs achieve parity with or surpass proxy metrics.
  • Few-shot learning further improves performance.
  • Clarifying serendipity in a scenario-aligned way improves LLM performance.

Key Findings

  • Auxiliary data (e.g., curiosity, similarity) boosts LLM evaluation performance for serendipity.
  • The impact of auxiliary data is domain-specific.

Conclusion

We conduct a meta-evaluation on two datasets derived from real user studies in the e-commerce and movie domains. Our findings imply that LLMs may serve as potentially accurate and cost-effective evaluators, introducing a new paradigm for serendipity evaluation in recommender systems.
